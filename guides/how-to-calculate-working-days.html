How to Calculate Working Days

UC Editorial Team
Published 2026-02-18 5 min read

Working days — sometimes called business days — exclude weekends (and often public holidays) from a date range. They matter for deadlines, delivery estimates, and payment terms, where "5 business days" means something different from "5 days."

The basic method

Count every calendar day between the start and end date, then subtract any Saturdays and Sundays that fall within that range. If public holidays should also be excluded, subtract those too — as long as they don't already fall on a weekend you've already removed.

Worked example

How many working days are there between Monday 3 August 2026 and Friday 14 August 2026 inclusive?

WeekWeekdays (Mon–Fri)Weekend days excluded
3–9 August5 (3rd–7th)2 (8th, 9th)
10–14 August5 (10th–14th)—

Total calendar days: 12. Weekend days removed: 2. Working days: 10.

A faster method for full weeks

For any date range, you can calculate working days quickly with this approach: divide the total number of days by 7 to get the number of full weeks (each contributing exactly 5 working days), then handle the remaining days individually by checking which fall on a weekend.

Working Days ≈ (Full Weeks × 5) + Weekday Remainder

Accounting for public holidays

Public holidays vary by country, region, and sometimes company, so no universal formula can include them automatically. If your working-day count needs to be exact for payroll, shipping, or legal deadlines, subtract each specific holiday that falls within your date range and isn't already on a weekend.

"5 business days from now" almost always means 5 working days starting the next working day, not including today. Confirm the exact convention with whoever set the deadline if it matters — conventions differ between "starting today" and "starting the next business day."

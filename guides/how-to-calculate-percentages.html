How to Calculate Percentages

UC Editorial Team
Published 2026-01-10 5 min read

Almost every percentage question people search for falls into one of three patterns. Once you recognize which one you're facing, the formula is simple.

1. Finding a percentage of a number

Question type: "What is 20% of 150?"

Result = (Percentage / 100) × Number

Example: (20 / 100) × 150 = 0.2 × 150 = 30.

2. Finding what percentage one number is of another

Question type: "45 is what percent of 300?"

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Example: (45 / 300) × 100 = 0.15 × 100 = 15%.

3. Finding the original number from a percentage

Question type: "30 is 20% of what number?"

Whole = Part / (Percentage / 100)

Example: 30 / (20 / 100) = 30 / 0.2 = 150.

Notice these three formulas are really the same relationship — Part = (Percentage/100) × Whole — just rearranged to solve for whichever value is unknown.

Percentage increase and decrease

To find the percentage change between two numbers:

% Change = ((New − Old) / Old) × 100

If a price rises from $80 to $92: ((92 − 80) / 80) × 100 = (12/80) × 100 = 15% increase. If it instead fell to $68: ((68 − 80) / 80) × 100 = −15%, i.e. a 15% decrease.

A common mistake worth avoiding

A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not return you to the original number. $100 increased by 50% is $150; $150 decreased by 50% is $75 — not $100. Percentage changes are always relative to the current value at the time, not the original one.

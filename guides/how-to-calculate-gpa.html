How to Calculate GPA

UC Editorial Team
Published 2026-03-01 6 min read

Grade Point Average (GPA) condenses every letter grade you've earned into a single number, weighted by how many credit hours each course was worth. Here's exactly how that number is built.

Step 1: Convert letter grades to grade points

Most US institutions use a 4.0 scale. A common conversion table looks like this:

Letter gradeGrade points
A4.0
A-3.7
B+3.3
B3.0
B-2.7
C+2.3
C2.0
D1.0
F0.0

Scales vary between schools, so check your institution's official table if you need an exact figure for your transcript.

Step 2: Weight each grade by credit hours

A course's contribution to your GPA depends on how many credit hours it's worth — a 4-credit course affects your GPA twice as much as a 2-credit course with the same grade.

GPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credit Hours) / Σ(Credit Hours)

Worked example

CourseGradeCreditsPoints × Credits
Calculus IA416.0
HistoryB+39.9
ChemistryB412.0
ElectiveA-27.4
Total1345.3

GPA = 45.3 / 13 = 3.48.

GPA vs CGPA

GPA typically refers to a single term or semester. CGPA (Cumulative GPA) is the same calculation applied across every term you've completed, using total grade points and total credit hours over your entire academic record so far.

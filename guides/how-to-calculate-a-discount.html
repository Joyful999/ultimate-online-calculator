How to Calculate a Discount

UC Editorial Team
Published 2026-01-22 4 min read

Working out a discount is one of the most common everyday percentage calculations — and one of the easiest to get wrong under pressure at a checkout. Here's a reliable method that works for any percentage off.

The formula

To find the sale price after a percentage discount:

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% / 100)

To find just the amount saved:

Amount Saved = Original Price × (Discount% / 100)

Worked example

A jacket costs $120 and is on sale at 25% off.

StepCalculationResult
Amount saved120 × 0.25$30.00
Sale price120 − 30$90.00

Stacked discounts aren't additive

If a store offers "25% off, plus an extra 10% off," it's tempting to add the two to get 35% off — but that's not how stacked discounts work. Each discount applies to the price after the previous one. On that same $120 jacket:

StepCalculationResult
After 25% off120 × 0.75$90.00
After extra 10% off90 × 0.90$81.00

The final price is $81, not the $78 you'd get by treating it as a flat 35% off ($120 × 0.65 = $78). The difference is small here, but grows with larger prices and steeper discounts.

Quick mental shortcut: to take 10% off any price, move the decimal point one place left. To take 25% off, divide by 4 and subtract. Combine these for fast estimates before you reach for a calculator.

Working backward from a sale price

If you know the sale price and the discount percentage but want the original price, rearrange the formula: Original Price = Sale Price ÷ (1 − Discount%/100). A $90 item marked as "25% off" had an original price of 90 ÷ 0.75 = $120.
